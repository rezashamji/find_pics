(1) ONE GLOBAL CUT OR PER KIND?  Per kind, at least object vs subject. No global value works.
  - Object questions ('Is there a real X anywhere ...'): keep 0.7. Precision is already 0.98-1.00. Moving to 0.99 costs car
    recall .713 -> .514 (~112 of 564 real photos) and bicycle .904 -> .654.
  - Subject questions ('Is this a photo of X?'): the best cut differs per subject. Beach only gets precise at P == 1.0
    (0.88; even 0.999999 gives only 0.81). Food is best at ~0.999999 (0.95 at recall .884). Sunset is best at 0.999-0.9999.
    Above 0.999 sunset, church and flowers lose many real photos. The planner writes an arbitrary X, so per-subject cuts
    would be tuned on 3 searches and cannot be applied to a new subject.
  RECOMMENDED: subject questions at logit gap >= 7.0 (P >= 0.999, one step stricter than today). Object questions stay at 0.7.
    Precision 0.99 -> 0.999: beach .62 -> .67, sunset .74 -> .83, food .60 -> .68.
    Recall cost (unsure = no match): beach 0, food 0, sunset .921 -> .884 (~5 of 138 real).
    Recall cost if unsure counts as a match: beach -.054, food -.032, sunset -.091.
    Other libraries: church loses 6 of 28 right photos, flowers 1 of 23, cat 0 of 11.

(2) FLOAT PRECISION
  - How the phone computes P (Judge.swift pOf): logits of the last position -> .asType(.float32) -> softmax over the whole
    vocabulary in float32 -> py = sum over {yes, Yes, ' yes', ' Yes'}, pn = same for no -> P = py / (py + pn) in Float32 ->
    Double. So P = 1 / (1 + e^-g) with g = logsumexp(yes logits) - logsumexp(no logits). The server does the same from
    vLLM's top-20 logprobs; the stored P is float32.
  - Two limits, and the coarser one is the logits, not P:
    (a) The logits arrive in bf16. In the stored data every g is a multiple of 0.25 (65 distinct values, step 0.250
        everywhere = bf16's spacing for logits of size 32-64). Each step multiplies 1-P by 1.28. The phone's MLX bf16 logits
        should show the same grid, but that is UNMEASURED. Kernel differences of a step or two move 1-P by 1.3-1.6x.
    (b) Float32 P saturates. Below 1.0 its steps are 2^-24 (6e-8), so float32 P == 1.0 means g >= 17.5 on the grid
        (exactly 1.0 once 1-P < 3e-8). From g ~ 14 up, the float32 rounding of P already bends the grid (14.56, 14.69,
        15.03 ...). The largest finite g stored is 16.64.
  - Photos with P exactly 1.0: beach 230, food 83, sunset 20; dog, car, bicycle 0 (the object question never gets above
    g 16.6). These photos are all lumped together: the data cannot tell g = 17.5 from g = 30.
  - Can the phone tell 0.99999 from 1.0? Yes. 0.99999 is g = 11.5, about 24 bf16 steps and 168 float32 steps below 1.0.
    Only cuts at or above ~0.9999999 (g ~ 16) sit inside the saturation zone.
  - Probability cuts land between grid steps. The level g = 11.5 has P = 0.9999899 < 0.99999, so 'P >= 0.99999' actually
    means g >= 11.75. 0.999 = g 6.91 -> first kept level 7.0. 0.9999 = 9.21 -> 9.25.
  - RECOMMENDATION: make the rule a logit-gap threshold. Compute g = logsumexp(yes logits) - logsumexp(no logits) in
    float32 directly from the last-position logits; this needs no full-vocabulary softmax. Keep on g >= T: object T = 0.85
    (= P 0.7), subject T = 6.91 (= P 0.999). On the 0.25 grid these keep the levels >= 1.0 and >= 7.0, the same photos as today's P test. The threshold is then readable and free of float32
    saturation, and it would let a future cut above 17 mean something. P stays for display.
    Before trusting any cut above ~12 on the device: log g on the phone for ~50 photos the cluster scored and compare
    (phone-vs-cluster logit agreement is unmeasured).
