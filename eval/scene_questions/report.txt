SCENE QUESTION WORDING (MAC_INBOX M44 (b)): can a different question fix "food / beach / sunset photos"?
Public DISBench data only. 2026-10-12. Code: eval/eval_scene_questions.py (gen / report / sheets).
Full tables: report_tables.txt. Per-photo P for every wording: p.tsv (7,886 photos x 3 searches; q0s = the stored
RESULTS 46 P). Rule-level numbers: rules.tsv. Other-library P: other_libraries_p.tsv.

SETUP
- Judge: Qwen3-VL-4B at the phone's REAL 4-bit weights (models/q3vl4b_real), the same pipeline that produced
  eval/recall_audit/scores_q3vl4b_real (eval_recall.py gen --model=models/q3vl4b_real): VLLMJudge + engine._judge_rows,
  native DISBench pixels (~500 px long side, nothing shrunk), P(yes) = py/(py+pn). Same prompt template; only the
  question string changes.
- Photos: ALL 7,886 photos of the 4 RESULTS 34 libraries, every wording (not just the labelled ones), plus 726 calls on
  RESULTS 35 (2) labelled photos in OTHER libraries (food, beach, sunset, flowers, cat, church; q0 + the two generic
  templates). 142,674 judge calls in total.
- Pipeline check: the current question re-judged here vs the stored RESULTS 46 P: keep decision at 0.999 is the same
  for 7,883/7,886 (beach), 7,884/7,886 (food), 7,883/7,886 (sunset). Logits are not bit-identical (vLLM batch order,
  bf16; median |logit-gap change| 0.125 = half the bf16 grid). Scored side by side: stored vs re-judged q0 at 0.999
  give the same precision/recall to 0.01.
- Wordings (X = food / a beach / a sunset):
    q0        "Is this a photo of X?"                                (current planner subject form; RESULTS 46 cut 0.999)
    g_main    "Is X the main subject of this photo?"                 (generic template)
    g_vis     "Is X clearly visible in this photo?"                  (generic template)
    f_meal    "Is food (a meal, dish or snack) the main subject of this photo?"
    f_served  "Does this photo show plated or served food up close?"
    b_sandy   "Is this photo taken on a sandy beach, with the sand or shoreline visible?"
    b_sandsea "Is a beach (sand next to the sea) clearly visible in this photo?"
    b_shore   "Is a beach (a sand or pebble shore by the sea or a lake) clearly visible in this photo?"
    s_sunsky  "Is the sun setting or the sky lit by a sunset visible in this photo?"
    s_sky     "Does the sky in this photo show a sunset (the sun low at the horizon, or the sky coloured orange or pink by it)?"
    two-question form: <wording> >= cut AND "Are people the main subject of this photo?" < 0.5 (food, beach).
- Truth: the RESULTS 34 estimator (eval_recall.score_systems; uniform blind draws inside the strata of the RESULTS 34
  judge; earlier labels exact) + the RESULTS 45 census as an exact stratum (= RESULTS 46 'V2') + 401 NEW blind eye labels
  (labels_extra.txt, 'V3' = main): every photo kept by any wording's best cut or by q0>=0.999 that had no label yet
  (food 51, beach 202, sunset 69) plus 79 decoys (unlabelled photos no rule kept, q0 P in [0.05, 0.999)), shuffled onto
  69 sheets at native pixels, labelled under eval/vector_enough/LABEL_PROTOCOL.txt before key_extra.json was read.
  With V3, 79-94% of every kept set below has an exact eye label (88-94% for the rules in the tables), so precision is close to a census; recall still rests
  on the RESULTS 34 strata (the Bayesian interval is the honest one: 0 misses seen in ~200 bulk photos still allows
  ~35 misses). 'unsure' = not a match (as RESULTS 34/45/46); unsure-as-match is in report_tables.txt.
- The eye labels are question-independent ("is this a food/beach/sunset photo" by the protocol), so they score any
  wording. All rules of one search are scored jointly (one shared total of real photos). Because V3 adds labels and
  finer cells, the q0 numbers here differ from RESULTS 46 (e.g. food q0>=0.999 recall .931 there, .957 here; precision
  .68 there, .70 here); compare rows within this report.
- Best cut: the cut in {0.7, 0.99, 0.999, 0.9999} with the highest F1 of the point estimates.

ANSWER
1. No single rewording of "Is this a photo of X?" is better for all three searches, and neither generic template is
   safe for every subject the planner might write. So: NOT a generic planner rule.
2. Per search (recommended):
   - BEACH: "Is a beach clearly visible in this photo?" at the existing subject cut 0.999. Precision .69 -> .85, recall
     .968 -> .949; exact changed photos: -40 no_match, -21 unsure, -6 real, +2 real. This is the one wording change that
     clearly pays. (Its best-F1 cut 0.9999 gives .87 / .926.)
   - FOOD: keep the wording, raise its cut to 0.9999 (logit gap 9.2): precision .70 -> .80, recall .957 -> .947 (drops
     19 photos, 1 real). Stricter wordings ("Is food the main subject...?" .94 / .805; "Is food (a meal, dish or snack)
     the main subject...?" .86 / .871) buy precision with 9-16 real food photos, mostly food with people in frame.
     A user-facing "strict" toggle could use g_main.
   - SUNSET: keep "Is this a photo of a sunset?" at 0.999. Nothing beats it outside noise.
3. Planner implications: these are three per-word exceptions, not a template. If adopted they belong in the same place
   as the subject-form rule (converse.ground() subject block ~line 505 and Grounding.swift ~line 473, which turn "X
   photos" into "Is this a photo of X?"): a small table keyed on the subject word (beach/beaches -> "Is a beach clearly
   visible in this photo?"), plus a per-word cut in JudgeCutoff (food 0.9999). Measured on ONE public set (4 Flickr
   libraries) for 3 words; any other scene word (mountains, snow, city at night, lake...) is unmeasured, so the table
   must not grow by guessing. Coordinator decides; nothing in the product was changed.

CAVEATS
- One set: 4 DISBench libraries, ~500 px photos. Recall intervals (Bayesian) are wide; the per-search differences above
  are carried by exactly-labelled changed photos (paired), which is stronger than the marginal intervals suggest.
- The 401 new labels were written by me, blind to judge and wording but knowing the sheets mixed kept photos with ~20%
  decoys. 80/401 are 'unsure' (mostly beach-trip close-ups, sand details, dusk skies with faint colour).
- 'unsure' counted as a match flips some conclusions toward the looser wordings: beach g_vis>=0.999 recall .819 vs q0
  .900; food g_main .494 vs q0 .687 (report_tables.txt, V3u column). The recommendation for beach holds with the
  protocol as written; a user who calls a sand close-up a "beach photo" would see it miss more.
- Phone: P on the device is from the same 4-bit weights; phone-vs-cluster logit agreement near 1.0 is still unmeasured
  (RESULTS 46 (4)); a 0.9999 cut on food needs float32 P or a logit-gap rule.
