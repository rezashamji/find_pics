M48: CAN A SMALL LEARNED HEAD OVER THE STORED EMBEDDINGS REPLACE THE PHOTO JUDGE? (2026-10-12; public data only)
Code: eval/learned_head/{embed,build,train_np,analyze,sheets}.py. Full tables: eval/learned_head/tables.txt.
Logs: train_E1_E1b_E2_E3_E4.log (its E2 section is SUPERSEDED, see E2 below), train_E2.log.

SHORT ANSWER
- For a query concept the head was TRAINED on (E4: same search seen on 16 other libraries), on 4 unseen libraries:
  pooled eye precision / recall at the judge's own count 0.88 / 0.85, vs the phone judge 0.93 / 0.90. Scene searches
  match the judge (beach .65 vs .68, food .66 vs .67, sunset .88 vs .89; dog .99 vs 1.00). Small objects do not
  (car .80 vs .98 precision, recall .58 vs .71; bicycle .56-.62 vs .98).
- For a query the head has NEVER seen (E1), it is no better than the phone's unlearned cosine: pooled eye precision /
  recall at the judge's count 0.80 / 0.79 vs cosine 0.81 / 0.80 vs judge 0.92 / 0.90. Remove the test libraries'
  photos from training (E1b) and the head falls BELOW cosine on teacher-relative AP (EV16 .62 vs .69).
- So: yes for scene categories / big subjects whose concept is in the training data; no for unseen concepts, small
  objects, relations, text, counting, and attribute-of-a-person questions. Stage 3 needs pixels for those.

MODELS (params; fp16 size)
  look   the phone's fast score exactly (mean look - mean avoid, whole vector; FindPicsCore lookScores). 0 params.
  cos_q  cosine(whole vector, judge-question text). 0 params.
  lr     logistic regression on [cos, img*txt]: 1,026 params.
  mlp    [img, txt, img*txt, |img-txt|] standardised -> 1024 -> 256 -> 1 (ReLU): 4,457,985 params, 8.9 MB fp16.
  att    same MLP on a query-attended image vector (softmax(50 cos(txt, v_j)) over whole + 4 tiles; fixed temperature):
         4,457,985 params; needs the 2x2 tile vectors the phone does NOT store today (RESULTS 42: +1.5 GB).
Training: soft teacher P where stored (JD, OI, A4), binary otherwise; each source weighted ~sqrt(its rows);
full-library sources subsampled to every teacher-yes + 500 hardest (cosine) + 3,000 random teacher-no per concept
(EV16 in E4: 200 + 300 per search x library). 6 epochs, Adam 1e-3 cosine, batch 1024. E1: 3 seeds (spread below).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Qwen3-VL-4B raw | .88 / .85 | .65 | .66 | .88 | .79 |
| (b) only photos where 4B and 9B agree | .88 / .86 | .65 | .66 | .88 | .80 |

Agreement filtering changes nothing measurable (only calibration: food at P >= 0.5 keeps 147 instead of 170, precision
.57 -> .66). Mechanism: the two judges share the same scene errors, so dropping disagreements removes few of them. The
head reproduces the judge's scene errors at the judge's rate (beach .65 vs .68) - it does not amplify them, but it
cannot fix them either. Seen at native pixels (sheets/fp_food.jpg): of the 6 eye-NO photos the head scores highest for
"food photos", 5 have judge P >= 0.96 (people eating cake at a party x3, a dessert menu, a dog at its bowl). Fixing
scene precision needs a better TARGET (stricter question / bigger judge / eye labels), not a filter on two similar
teachers.

WHAT THE HEAD CANNOT EXPRESS (viewed at native ~500 px, eval/learned_head/sheets/, index.tsv)
- Small / peripheral objects (miss_car.jpg, miss_bicycle.jpg): eye-real cars as specks on a causeway (judge P .997,
  head .039), a red car at the top edge of a cemetery photo (judge .378, head .040), aerial harbour streets; bicycles
  partly hidden behind a girl at the frame edge (judge .986, head .044), parked behind a street-food stall (judge .986,
  head .093), leaning on a blue house (judge 1.000, head .131). One 1024-d whole-photo vector does not carry a
  20-pixel bicycle. Tiles help a little (att: A4 bicycle eye AUC .922 -> .959, car .827 -> .872) and cost +1.5 GB.
  Dogs that small are missed by the judge too (miss_dog.jpg: 5 of 6 have judge P <= 0.12).
- Attribute bound to a person (jd_personlook.jpg): "Is the person wearing a blue shirt?" teacher YES on a man in a teal
  shirt, head .175; teacher NO on a man in red HOLDING a blue shirt, head .537 - the vector says "blue shirt present",
  not whose. "Are people wearing matching shirts?" head .31 on a matching pair, .57 on a mismatched pair.
- Counting / comparison (jd_count.jpg): "two children who look like twins": head .60 on two different boys, .38 on the
  twin babies. "birthday cake with two or three candles": head .69 on two sparklers (teacher NO), .57 on the real one.
- Relations (jd_relation.jpg): "person standing behind a child": head .18 on the real case, .33 on a bronze statue.
  "Is the person holding a fish?": head .18 on a man holding a fish, .24 on an aquarium fish with no person.
- Text in the image (jd_text.jpg): the head cannot read; AUC among top candidates .70-.72.
- Identity is out of scope here (faces have their own vectors; not tested).

PHONE BUDGET (mlp; ESTIMATES, nothing run on a device)
- 4,457,985 params = 8.9 MB fp16. Per (photo, query): 2 x (4096x1024 + 1024x256 + 256) = 8.9 MFLOP.
  187k photos: 1.67 TFLOP per search. Rewritten exactly: layer 1 = A img + B txt + C (img*txt) + D |img-txt|; per search
  fold C diag(txt) + A into one 1024x1024 matrix, so per photo ~4.7 MFLOP (0.88 TFLOP / 187k); retrained without the
  |img-txt| block, ~2.6 MFLOP (0.49 TFLOP).
- ESTIMATE: A17 Pro Neural Engine is rated 35 TOPS by Apple (int8); at fp16 and 10-30% utilisation 0.5-1.7 TFLOP is
  ~0.1-0.5 s; on the GPU (~2 TFLOPS fp32) ~0.5-2 s. Plus reading the 0.38 GB vector store (cold ~0.2-0.4 s, RESULTS 45
  estimate). M-class Macs: faster. Compare the judge: 2-5 s PER PHOTO.
- att adds 4 tile vectors per photo (RESULTS 42: +1.53 GB store, +2-8 h indexing) and ~1.5x the FLOPs.
- lr (1,026 params) is one dot product per photo but is WORSE than the phone's cosine on held-out queries (EV16 AP .54 vs .69).

RECOMMENDATION
1. The head is a better ranker, not a judge replacement for unseen queries. The one regime where it matches the judge
   is "concept seen in training, scene-level or big subject". That is a data question: E1 -> E4 (same model, the
   concept added via 16 other libraries) moved pooled eye precision at the judge's count .80 -> .88 and car .50 -> .80.
   Next step with GPUs: generate full-library teacher labels (not 24 best + 24 random) for the concepts people actually
   type: ~10^4 planner questions x ~500 photos (300 hardest by cosine + 200 random) = ~5M pairs. At RESULTS 26's
   ~50 photos/s per A100 (exhaustive 1,900-photo library in ~37 s) that is ~28 GPU-hours = ~2 h on 16 GPUs. Use the
   stricter scene questions (RESULTS 46) for the labels, since agreement filtering does not remove shared errors.
2. Then measure what matters for the product: replace cosine with the head as fast mode's RANKING (same judge,
   RESULTS 41 stop rule replay) and count judge calls saved at equal recall; and let the head answer alone only for
   searches whose concept is in its training set and is scene-level (firstScreenDecision-style gate).
3. Small objects, relations, counting, text and person attributes need pixels: keep the judge for them (or a
   crop-level model; tiles are a partial, expensive fix). "Stage 3 needs pixels" holds for exactly those kinds.
