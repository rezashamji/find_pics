M46 SELFIE DETECTOR WITHOUT EXIF: public-data fit and evaluation (cluster, 2026-10-12)
Public data only. Nothing from data/private was read.

QUESTION
Can the phone tell "front-camera photo" from "back-camera photo" without the original's EXIF, using only:
(a) the PE-Core-B-16 image vector it already holds for every photo, and/or
(b) face boxes (geometry relative to the frame)?
The goal is a "selfies" answer with zero judge calls and zero downloads for the ~170k iCloud-only photos.

1. LABELLED PUBLIC SET
- The local public sets have no EXIF. DISBench / everyday16 and Open Images are Flickr resizes: 0/150 sampled
  files from each had any EXIF. DISBench metadata gives only "capturedevice" (camera model), never the lens.
- Source used instead: Wikimedia Commons, which keeps the original's EXIF and serves it through the API.
  MediaWiki drops EXIF LensModel. So the label is (Model, FocalLength, FNumber): each iPhone's front camera has its
  own focal length and aperture. These were identified on the harvest table:
  - front: 2.6-2.9 mm at f/2.2 (iPhone 6 to 13, SE 2/3); 2.0-3.0 mm at f/1.9 (14 on); 2.15/2.18 mm or 1.85 mm at
    f/2.4 (5/5s/SE1, 4/4S).
  - back: every lens of 3.5 mm or more, the 1.5-1.6 mm ultra-wides, and 2.22 mm f/2.2. That last lens is the
    ultra-wide of 14 Pro to 17, including the 16 and 17 non-Pro: 87 of 247 iPhone 16 photos use it, far above any
    front-camera share.
  - Any triple that matches neither rule is dropped, never guessed: 8,858 files.
- Harvest. 74 "Taken with iPhone ..." categories (up to 15k files each), the Category:Selfies tree (up to 400
  subcategories), and Commons searches:
  - by front-camera pixel size (3088x2316 or 3088x2320 is the 7-MP front camera of iPhone 7 to XR / SE 2-3);
  - by the words "selfie" and "self-portrait".
  This gave 183,721 files, 169,683 of them Apple. 163,902 got a label, and 3,028 of those were front.
  In the categories alone, front shots are rare: about 560 of about 170k.
- Kept for fitting:
  - every front photo: 3,028 from 1,920 uploaders, 49 iPhone models, 2012-2026;
  - MODEL-MATCHED back photos: up to 5 per front photo of the same model, 14,566 in total. Without this the
    detector could win by spotting camera generation, because the backs were mostly 3GS/4/4S;
  - at most 150 photos per uploader per label;
  - 17,594 photos in total, from 4,244 uploaders.
  - Each photo is a 1280-px Commons thumbnail. Commons applies the EXIF rotation, as the phone does.
- Eye check of the tag at native thumbnail size, on 7 sheets of 6 per class:
  - front-tagged, 42 random: 36/42 are selfies (the photographer's own face at arm's length, or a propped or
    timer self-portrait). 6/42 are not: 3 ceilings or buildings shot looking up, a hand on a guitar, a chest
    wound, a crowd.
  - back-tagged, 42 random: 0/42 are selfies. 41/42 are scenes or objects; 1/42 is a close portrait of someone
    else.
  - So "front camera" is a good but imperfect proxy for "selfie" (36/42). Commons back photos contain far fewer
    people than a personal camera roll does (see caveats).

2. FEATURES (GPU job; scripts below)
- PE-Core-B-16 image vector:
  - open_clip hf-hub:timm/PE-Core-B-16, squash to 224 px, L2-normalized: the same model and preprocessing as
    the phone's Core ML tower (Embedder.swift).
  - Not reused from existing indexes: none of the public indexes has EXIF labels.
- Face boxes: findpics.models.FaceEncoder("auraface_flip"), SCRFD-10G detector with det_thresh 0.5, run on the
  1280-px image.
- Photos with at least one face:
  - front: 2,736/3,028
  - back: 3,036/14,566

3. RESULTS
Setup:
- 5-fold GroupKFold BY UPLOADER: no uploader is ever in both train and test.
- Every number comes from held-out users.
- 95% CIs come from an uploader-cluster bootstrap (1,000 resamples for AUC, 500 for precision and recall).
- Operating points are picked HONESTLY: the threshold for "precision >= p" is chosen on inner grouped CV of each
  outer-train fold, then applied to the held-out fold.

Methods:
- geo_rule = one threshold on the largest face's area / frame area.
- geo_lr   = logistic regression on six geometry features:
  sqrt(area), centre offset, centre y, log(1 + n faces), 2nd/1st face area, has-face.
- vec_lr   = logistic regression on the 1024-d vector (L2 penalty, C=0.005 chosen by grouped CV).
- combo_lr = vec_lr's input plus the geometry features.

AUC, all photos (3,028 pos / 14,566 neg):
  geo_rule  0.932 [0.914, 0.945]   face subset 0.946   no-face subset 0.50 (no face: no signal)
  geo_lr    0.929 [0.903, 0.947]   face subset 0.949   no-face subset 0.43
  vec_lr    0.994 [0.992, 0.995]   face subset 0.991   no-face subset 0.975
  combo_lr  0.994 [0.992, 0.995]   face subset 0.992   no-face subset 0.975

Operating point, target precision >= 0.90 (threshold picked on train folds):

  method    precision [CI]       recall [CI]          TP/pos      FP/neg
  geo_rule  0.896 [0.876, 0.917]  0.828 [0.793, 0.857]  2508/3028   290/14566
  geo_lr    0.897 [0.877, 0.916]  0.835 [0.802, 0.863]  2529/3028   290/14566
  vec_lr    0.901 [0.878, 0.919]  0.944 [0.931, 0.957]  2859/3028   315/14566

  No-face subset: the geometry methods find 0/292 front photos (0/11,530 false positives); vec_lr finds 186/292

4. EYE AUDIT OF vec_lr (out-of-fold threshold -0.726, the precision-0.90 point). Sheets were viewed, not inferred.
- Top 24 false positives (back-tagged, high score): 12/24 look like selfies by eye: a close face at arm's length,
  a car selfie, a mirror selfie. Several are titled "selfie" or filed in Category:Selfies, but their EXIF lens is
  a back lens. The other 12/24 are back-camera portraits of someone else: close head shots, studio portraits, a
  group pose, the ISS crew, a medical close-up.
  So about half of the "errors" at the top are selfie-looking photos taken with the back camera, or mirror selfies.
  A user asking for "selfies" would accept those.
- 18 random misses (front-tagged, low score): 2/18 are selfies (a couple in helmets with pixelated faces; a half
  face in a cable car). 16/18 are not selfies by eye: front camera used as a normal camera (landscapes, a spiral
  staircase, a cockpit, crowds, a dog in a car window, a fist, posed or timer portraits).
  So the misses are mostly where the EXIF tag and "selfie" disagree.
- 12 random hits: 11/12 are clear selfies; 1/12 is a dog on a boat shot with the front camera.

5. WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE PHONE

Precision depends on how common selfies are among the photos being searched. Commons is not a camera roll.

Bare "selfies" over the whole library:
- Assume selfies are 1.6% of the library: 267 front-tagged out of about 17k originals on the device.
- At logit 0.5 (recall 0.90):
  - FPR from the Commons back-photo mix (0.84%) gives precision 0.64.
  - Weighting by Reza's face share (42.5% of photos have faces), with face FPR 3.3% and no-face FPR about 0.24%,
    gives precision about 0.49.
- So on about 170k untagged photos, expect roughly 2,500 true front photos returned with about 1,400 to 2,600
  back-camera photos alongside them. About half of the false ones are likely to look like selfies anyway (audit
  above).

"My selfies" (scored inside the owner-face set from the M42 face filter):
- Assumption: selfies are a large share of photos of oneself.
- Using face-subset rates at logit 0.5 (recall 0.947, FPR 0.033): if selfies are 10% of that set, precision
  0.76; at 20%, 0.88; at 40%, 0.95.

Verdict:
- vec_lr is good enough to replace the judge as the RANKING stage for selfies: zero downloads, a dot product per
  photo.
- It is good enough to return "my selfies" directly when combined with the face filter.
- It is NOT good enough to call a bare whole-library "selfies" list precise without a check: about 50-64% of
  results would carry the front tag. Ship it as the instant answer ranked by score, and label it on screen
  as "looks like a selfie".
- Judge verification is optional and best limited to the score band near the threshold.
- Do not use face geometry alone: AUC 0.93, it cannot reach precision 0.95, and it misses every selfie with no
  detected face.
- Adding geometry to the vector gains nothing measurable (0.994 vs 0.994). So the phone needs no geometry
  thresholds, which also avoids the detector mismatch below.

6. FIT ON THE PHONE, OR SHIP WEIGHTS?
Recommendation: ship the cluster-fitted vec_lr weights and only VALIDATE on the phone.
- The weights are a 1024-d weight vector plus a bias. weights.json -> vec_lr.w_folded and vec_lr.b_folded:
  score = dot(w_folded, v) + b_folded, where v is the stored L2-normalized PE-Core-B-16 vector.
- On the phone:
  - score the 267 front-tagged and the back-tagged originals;
  - report AUC and recall/FPR at logit 0.5;
  - optionally re-pick that one threshold on the device.
  The owner's data never leaves the phone.

Why not fit on the device, even though the learning curve says 267 positives would be enough on Commons
(0.991 vs 0.994)?
(a) That curve has 267 positives from about 1,900 different people. The phone's 267 come from essentially one
    person, so an on-device fit is likely to learn "Reza's face / Reza's rooms" instead of "front camera". That
    would collapse into a weaker copy of the face filter. This cannot be measured with one person's data, and the
    public fit avoids the risk.
(b) The tagged subset is biased: recent originals, one or two phone models, recent places. A fit there would
    overweight those. Validation on it is still informative, but read its numbers as recent-photo numbers.
(c) Fitting needs training code and a choice of negatives on the device; validation needs only a dot product.

7. CAVEATS (not measured here)
- Commons back-camera photos are mostly scenes. Only 3,036/14,566 have a face, against about 42.5% in Reza's
  library, and personal libraries have more close portraits of family and friends. The face-subset FPR (3.3% at
  logit 0.5) is the number to trust for people photos, and the phone validation will show the real one.
- The vectors here are server PE-Core-B-16 on 1280-px thumbnails. The phone's are Core ML fp16 on the photo
  (cosine about 0.995 to the server per Embedder.swift). Scores may shift slightly: re-pick the threshold on the
  device.
- Face boxes here are SCRFD. The phone uses Apple Vision boxes, whose sizes differ. The recommended detector does
  not use boxes; if geometry is ever used, its threshold must be re-fit on Vision boxes.
- Few iPhone 17/18 front photos are in the set (17 Pro Max: 2). iPhone 14-17 together: 453 front photos, AUC
  0.993.
- "Front camera" is not the same as "selfie": 36/42 front shots are selfies, and back-camera and mirror selfies
  exist. Whether the product question should be "front camera" or "looks like a selfie" is a product decision.
  The vector detector already behaves closer to "looks like a selfie".

FOLLOW-UP 10-12: WHY THE 0.5 CUT FAILED ON THE PHONE (M50 on device: AUC 0.963 on 581 front / 1,671 back tagged photos;
logit 0.5 gave a 23% false-positive rate; at 95% precision the recall was 0.46). Public data only.

HYPOTHESIS TESTED: the fit used vectors of 1,280 px thumbnails, but the phone stores vectors of its 448 px rendition.
HYPOTHESIS REFUTED: the rendition is not the cause.
- Phone simulation of all 17,594 Commons photos (eval/selfie_detector/extract_phone_sim.py):
  long side 448 (Lanczos), JPEG round trip q80 4:2:0, then open_clip's squash to 224 and the fp16 PE-Core-B-16 tower.
  Variants: 480 q80, 448 q95 4:4:4, 448 with no JPEG. I viewed 3 renditions at native size; they look like the phone's.
- Cosine of these vectors to the 1,280 px vector: q80 0.941 mean (p5 0.917); q95 4:4:4 0.988; no JPEG 0.994.
  So the vectors DO move, mostly because of the JPEG.
- But the M50 detector barely notices. Below, the old weights are fitted per fold on 1,280 px and score the held-out
  uploaders' vectors (FPR = share of back photos called selfies):

DECISIONS
- weights_phone.json: same layout as weights.json. vec_lr = the 448 q80 fit:
  score = dot(w_folded, v) + b_folded on the stored vector.
  - Out-of-fold Commons thresholds: precision 0.90 at -0.66, precision 0.95 at 0.34.
  - vec_lr_alt = the stacked fit; combo_lr, geo_lr and geo_rule are refits.
  - Switching to it is principled (it matches the phone's input) but should not change the phone's numbers much:
    within face >= 5% its AUC is 0.959 against 0.960 for the old weights.
- Per-library calibration on the phone (PhotoIndex.selfieThreshold): KEEP. It is not just a safety net, it is
  required. The right cut depends on what share of a library's back-camera photos are close portraits, and that
  varies from person to person; Commons cannot predict it. A shipped cut would have been wrong by about 4 logits on
  Reza's library.
- Expect the same on-device behaviour with the new weights: AUC about 0.96, recall about 0.45-0.5 at 95% precision.
- The lever for more recall at 95% precision is many more back-camera chest-up portraits as negatives. Commons has
  only 282 in this set, so they need another source; I have not done this.
